Everyone carries a dream in their heart, and I am no exception. I’ve long cherished a dream that deeply inspires me: becoming an art film producer.

Some might think that being an AI developer and producing art films is an unlikely match. However, the two are profoundly connected. Creativity lies at the very core of both fields, and art cinema serves as a powerful catalyst, sparking the creative energy essential for AI development.

Life is meant to be more than just work. We need values that enrich our lives and prompt us to ask: What can I contribute to society? As an AI developer and film producer, I want to dedicate my work to making the world a warmer place.

As a concrete step toward this goal, I have laid out a roadmap at mcorpai.kr to support children battling rare illnesses through art films. The guiding principle of my life is the public good. I invite you to join me on this journey and amplify this positive impact.

## Vision Statement

When establishing an AI research lab, visibility is essential—but traditional marketing often feels transactional and wasteful. My goal is to redefine how promotional capital is used: instead of pouring budget into conventional advertising, we invest those funds into producing high-quality art films.

All proceeds from these films will go directly toward supporting children battling rare and incurable illnesses. By turning marketing spend into meaningful art and real-world relief, we build a self-sustaining cycle where cutting-edge AI research fuels artistic storytelling, and cinema directly enriches lives. It is marketing reimagined through public good—bringing recognition to our lab while making the world a genuinely warmer place.


## The Common Good as a Foundation of Security

I believe that the essence of security lies not simply in the technical ability to block attacks and protect systems, but in **the authenticity of our commitment to the Common Good**.

When an attacker attempts to compromise a system or cause harm to others, security serves as a defensive force—protecting people, systems, and the information they depend on. But if security is reduced to technical defense alone, we can lose sight of a more fundamental question: **What are we protecting, and whom are we protecting it for?**

To fulfill its purpose, security requires more than technical expertise. It requires a genuine commitment to values such as **human safety, freedom, dignity, and the Common Good**.

Ultimately, I believe security is not merely a technological issue. It is also a question of **what we choose to protect and why we choose to use technology to protect it**. Even the most advanced technology can become a tool for control or oppression if it is not guided by a commitment to the Common Good. Conversely, security technology grounded in that principle can serve as a genuine safeguard for people's safety and freedom.

One day, I hope to establish a small security research institute and build the foundation necessary to achieve financial independence through its work. However, the financial independence I seek is not simply about accumulating personal wealth. **I want to be economically independent so that I can conduct research and act according to my convictions without being unduly influenced by external interests.**

If I am given that opportunity, I want to devote my skills, creativity, and technical abilities not merely to personal success, but to **developing security technologies that protect people's safety, freedom, and dignity and contribute meaningfully to the Common Good**.

The security I aspire to build is not merely security that stops attacks. It is **security that protects people—and security that exists in service of the Common Good**.
